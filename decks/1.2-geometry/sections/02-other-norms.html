Act 2 / Three norms

One vector, three lengths

NormRule\(\vec v = [3, 2]\)
L1\(\lVert \vec v \rVert_1 = \sum_i |v_i|\)3 + 2 = 5
L2\(\lVert \vec v \rVert_2 = \sqrt{\sum_i v_i^2}\)√13 ≈ 3.61
L∞\(\lVert \vec v \rVert_\infty = \max_i |v_i|\)max(3, 2) = 3

Always \(L_\infty \le L_2 \le L_1\). Which one you use is a modelling choice.

Act 2 / Why the max

Why \(p \to \infty\) picks the largest entry

Let \(|x_k|\) be the largest entry. Pull \(|x_k|^p\) out of the sum:

$$\lVert \vec x \rVert_p = \Big(|x_k|^p \sum_i \Big(\tfrac{|x_i|}{|x_k|}\Big)^p\Big)^{1/p} = |x_k| \Big(1 + \sum_{i \ne k} \Big(\tfrac{|x_i|}{|x_k|}\Big)^p\Big)^{1/p}$$

Every ratio is at most 1, so the bracket stays between 1 and \(n\). Raising it to \(1/p\) sends it to 1, since \(n^{1/p} \to 1\).

$$\boxed{\lVert \vec x \rVert_\infty = \lim_{p \to \infty} \lVert \vec x \rVert_p = \max_i |x_i|}$$

AdaMax (Kingma and Ba, 2015) uses this limit: it swaps Adam's average of squared gradients for a running max of \(|g|\).

Act 2 / Where this shows up

Penalising weights is a norm

Ridge

Loss \(+ \lambda \lVert \vec w \rVert_2^2\). Shrinks every weight a little.

Lasso

Loss \(+ \lambda \lVert \vec w \rVert_1\). Pushes many weights to exactly zero.

Weights \(\vec w = [0.5, -1, 2]\) (illustrative values):

\(\lVert \vec w \rVert_1 = 0.5 + 1 + 2 = 3.5\)

\(\lVert \vec w \rVert_2^2 = 0.25 + 1 + 4 = 5.25\)
