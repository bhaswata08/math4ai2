Length

What does it mean for a vector to have a length? A city walker and a crow answer differently.

The city walker and the crow

The walker cannot cut through buildings: \(3 + 2 = 5\) blocks. The crow flies straight: \(\sqrt{3^2 + 2^2} \approx 3.61\). Same arrow, two lengths.

The crow's shortcut is Pythagoras

The x part and the y part are the legs of a right triangle. \(\lVert \vec v \rVert = \sqrt{3^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{13}\).

Act 1 / Two travelers

Two rules, any number of entries

City walker · L1 · Manhattan

Steps taken strictly along the axes. \(\lVert \vec v \rVert_1 = |v_1| + |v_2| + \cdots + |v_n|\)

Crow · L2 · Euclidean

Ruler distance, the same however you turn the grid. \(\lVert \vec v \rVert_2 = \sqrt{v_1^2 + \cdots + v_n^2} = \sqrt{\vec v \cdot \vec v}\)

For \(\vec v = [1, -2, 2]\): \(L_1 = 1 + 2 + 2 = \textcolor{#f0a35e}{5}\), \(\;L_2 = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 4} = \textcolor{#f0a35e}{3}\).

L1 takes absolute values: two blocks backwards are still two blocks walked.
