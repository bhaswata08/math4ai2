Orthogonal and orthonormal

Perpendicular, unit-length directions make the best coordinate axes.

Orthogonal means dot product zero

\([2, 1]\) and \([-1, 2]\): \(-2 + 2 = 0\), so they are perpendicular.

Act 6 / Three words

Orthogonal, unit, orthonormal

Orthogonal

\(\vec v \cdot \vec w = 0\). Perpendicular, \(\cos\theta = 0\).

Unit

\(\lVert \vec u \rVert = 1\). Normalized length.

Orthonormal

Orthogonal to each other, and every one has length 1.

Coordinates are dot products

In an orthonormal basis, the coordinates of \(\vec x\) are \(\vec x \cdot \vec u_1\) and \(\vec x \cdot \vec u_2\).

Act 6 / Example

Coordinates of [2, 1] in a rotated basis

$$\vec u_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.6 \\ 0.8 \end{bmatrix}, \; \vec u_2 = \begin{bmatrix} -0.8 \\ 0.6 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\vec x \cdot \vec u_1 = 1.2 + 0.8 = \textcolor{#f0a35e}{2}, \qquad \vec x \cdot \vec u_2 = -1.6 + 0.6 = \textcolor{#f0a35e}{-1}$$

Check: \(\vec u_1 \cdot \vec u_2 = -0.48 + 0.48 = 0\), both have length 1, and \(2\vec u_1 - \vec u_2 = [2, 1]\).
