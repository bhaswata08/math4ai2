The learning rate

Too small wastes time. Too large never arrives.

Three learning rates on \(L(w) = w^2\)

The update is \(w_{k+1} = w_k - \eta\cdot 2w_k = (1-2\eta)\,w_k\). \(\eta = 0.05\) gives factor \(0.90\): crawls. \(\eta = 0.30\) gives \(0.40\): converges. \(\eta = 1.05\) gives \(-1.10\): grows 10% per step and flips sign.

Act 2 / Why 1.05 diverges

Each step multiplies \(w\) by a constant

$$w_{k+1} = w_k - \eta \cdot 2w_k = (1 - 2\eta)\,w_k$$

Near a minimum \(\mathbf w^*\), a loss looks like a bowl. Let \(\lambda_{\max}\) be the largest eigenvalue of the Hessian: the steepest curvature. In the steepest direction \(L \approx L^* + \tfrac12\lambda\,(w - w^*)^2\), so a step gives $$w_{k+1} - w^* = (1 - \eta\lambda)\,(w_k - w^*)$$ The gap shrinks when \(|1 - \eta\lambda| < 1\), i.e. \(0 < \eta < 2/\lambda\). The steepest direction is the strictest, so \(\eta < 2/\lambda_{\max}\). For \(L = w^2\), \(\lambda = 2\), giving \(\eta < 1\).

Shrinks

\(0 < \eta < 0.5\): factor in \((0, 1)\): shrinks without crossing zero.

Flips, shrinks

\(0.5 < \eta < 1\): factor in \((-1, 0)\): flips sign but still shrinks.

Grows

\(\eta > 1\): \(|1 - 2\eta| > 1\): every step grows. At 1.05 the factor is \(-1.1\).
