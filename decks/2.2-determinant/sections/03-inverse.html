Act 3 / Definition

The inverse matrix

$$A^{-1}A = AA^{-1} = I$$

Doing \(A\) and then \(A^{-1}\) is the same as doing nothing.

\(A^{-1}\) exists exactly when \(\det A \neq 0\).

Act 3 / 2×2

Inverse of a 2×2

$$\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}^{-1} = \frac{1}{ad-bc}\begin{bmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{bmatrix}$$

Swap \(a\) and \(d\), negate \(b\) and \(c\), divide by the determinant.

\(\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\2&2\end{bmatrix}^{-1} = \tfrac14\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\-2&3\end{bmatrix}\), since \(\det = 6 - 2 = 4\).

No way back

\(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\) sends many inputs to one output. Nothing can undo that.

The zero matrix

The extreme case: every point lands on the origin.

Squashing onto a line or plane

Each of these maps flattens space, so none has an inverse.

Act 3 / Singular

When there is no inverse

\(\det A \ne 0\)

Invertible. Nothing is lost, every output has one input.

\(\det A = 0\)

Singular. Space is flattened, and the lost information is gone.

Act 3 / Product

Socks and shoes

$$(AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1}$$

Put on socks (\(B\)), then shoes (\(A\)). To undo, take off the shoes first, then the socks.

Act 3 / Solving

Solving \(A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}\)

With \(A = \begin{bmatrix}3&1\\2&2\end{bmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix}5\\6\end{bmatrix}\):

$$\mathbf{x} = A^{-1}\mathbf{b} = \tfrac14\begin{bmatrix}2&-1\\-2&3\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5\\6\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}1\\2\end{bmatrix}$$

Check: \(3 + 2 = 5\) and \(2 + 4 = 6\).

In code, call np.linalg.solve(A, b). Do not form inv(A): it is slower and less accurate.
