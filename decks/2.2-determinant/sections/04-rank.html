Rank

How many dimensions survive the map.

Rank is the dimension of the output

The plane stays a plane (rank 2), collapses to a line (rank 1), then to a point (rank 0).

Act 4 / Definition

Column space and rank

The column space is every output \(A\mathbf{x}\): all combinations of the columns of \(A\).

$$\operatorname{rank} A = \dim(\text{column space}) = \text{number of independent columns}$$

This is the span and independence of Blocks 1.3 and 1.4, applied to the columns.

Act 4 / Example

A rank-2 matrix

$$A = \begin{bmatrix}1&0&1\\0&1&1\\1&1&2\end{bmatrix}$$

The third column is the sum of the first two, so only two columns are independent. Rank 2, and \(\det A = 0\): the flat cube from the volume clip.

Act 4 / Square matrices

Three questions, one answer

For an \(n\times n\) matrix \(A\), these are equivalent:

  • \(A\) is invertible
  • \(\operatorname{rank} A = n\) (full rank)
  • \(\det A \neq 0\)
Act 4 / Rank–nullity

What is lost

$$n = \operatorname{rank} A + \dim(\text{null space})$$

The null space holds every input that \(A\) sends to \(\mathbf{0}\). For the matrix above, \(A(1,1,-1)^{\mathsf T} = \mathbf{0}\), so the null space is a line: \(3 = 2 + 1\).

What survives plus what vanishes always adds up to the input dimension.

Act 4 / In AI

Low-rank updates: LoRA

Fine-tuning a layer \(W \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\) by learning a change \(\Delta W\) directly costs \(d^2\) numbers. LoRA restricts the change to low rank:

$$\Delta W = BA,\quad B \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times r},\; A \in \mathbb{R}^{r\times d},\; r \ll d$$

That is \(2dr\) numbers. For \(d = 4096\) and \(r = 8\): about 65 thousand instead of 16.8 million.
