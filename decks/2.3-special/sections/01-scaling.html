Act 1 of 7

Scaling, reflection, shear

Three moves that cover most of the matrices you will meet.

25 min

A scalar matrix shrinks everything

\(\begin{bmatrix}0.5&0\\0&0.5\end{bmatrix}\) pulls every dot halfway to the origin.

The same idea in 3D

\(2I\) doubles every length. The cube keeps its shape and grows.

Scale one axis only

\(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&3\end{bmatrix}\): watch the dots move along y. The x coordinate never changes.

Squeeze along x

\(\begin{bmatrix}0.5&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\): an entry below 1 squeezes that axis.

Diagonal: each axis gets its own factor

\(\begin{bmatrix}3&0\\0&2\end{bmatrix}\) stretches x by 3 and y by 2.

Diagonal in 3D

\(\mathrm{diag}(3,\,2,\,0.5)\) turns the cube into a box: longer, wider, flatter.

Act 1 / Diagonal

A diagonal matrix is a stack of one-axis scalings

$$\begin{bmatrix}3&0\\0&2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}3x\\2y\end{bmatrix} \qquad \begin{bmatrix}3&0\\0&2\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}3&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{bmatrix}$$

Each coordinate is scaled on its own, so the order of the factors does not matter.

The determinant is the product of the diagonal: the area scales by \(3\cdot 2 = 6\).

Flip across the y-axis

\(\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\): the sign of x flips, so the rectangle jumps to the left.

Flip across the x-axis

\(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}\): now the sign of y flips.

Flip both signs

\(-I\) reflects through the origin. In 2D this is the same as a half turn.

Act 1 / Negative entries

A negative diagonal entry reflects

$$\begin{bmatrix}-2.5&0\\0&0.6\end{bmatrix}= \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}}_{\text{reflect}} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&0.6\end{bmatrix}}_{\text{scale } y} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}2.5&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}}_{\text{scale } x}$$

Read a diagonal matrix entry by entry: size is the stretch, sign is the flip. A reflection has determinant \(-1\).

A shear slants the plane

\(\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix}\): the top moves right, the bottom moves left, the rectangle becomes a parallelogram.

Four shears

One off-diagonal entry, either sign, in either position: four ways to slant.

A shear in 3D

Layers of the cube slide past each other, parallel to a plane.

Act 1 / Shear keeps area

Slanting does not change the area

The base of the rectangle stays the same. The height stays the same. Only the sides lean.

$$\det\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix}=1$$

Scalar: shape kept, area changed.

Shear: shape changed, area kept.

Scaling and reflection are diagonal. A shear has a single off-diagonal entry and ones on the diagonal.
