Jennifer's grid

Same origin, different grid lines. Her first steps are \(\vec b_1 = [2, 1]\) and \(\vec b_2 = [-1, 1]\).

From her numbers to ours

Jennifer says \([-1, 2]\). Build \(-1\,\vec b_1 + 2\,\vec b_2\) on her grid, then read the tip on ours.

Act 3 / Her numbers to ours

Scale her basis vectors, then add

$$-1\textcolor{#6fd0c0}{\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}} + 2\textcolor{#c792ea}{\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 - 2 \\ -1 + 2 \end{bmatrix} = \textcolor{#f0a35e}{\begin{bmatrix} -4 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}}$$

Her \([-1, 2]\) is our \([-4, 1]\). Day 2 packs this recipe into the change-of-basis matrix.

From our numbers to hers

We say \([3, 2]\). Find \(a, b\) with \(a\,\vec b_1 + b\,\vec b_2 = [3, 2]\).

Act 3 / Our numbers to hers

Two equations, two unknowns

$$a\textcolor{#6fd0c0}{\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}} + b\textcolor{#c792ea}{\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \;\Longrightarrow\; \begin{aligned} 2a - b &= 3 \\ a + b &= 2 \end{aligned}$$
$$3a = 5 \;\Rightarrow\; \textcolor{#f0a35e}{a = \tfrac53},\quad \textcolor{#f0a35e}{b = 2 - \tfrac53 = \tfrac13}$$

Check: \(\tfrac53[2,1] + \tfrac13[-1,1] = [3, 2]\). Her coordinates are \((\tfrac53, \tfrac13)\).

Act 3 / A better basis

Orthonormal: coordinates are dot products

$$\vec x = (\vec x\cdot\textcolor{#6fd0c0}{\vec u_1})\,\textcolor{#6fd0c0}{\vec u_1} + (\vec x\cdot\textcolor{#c792ea}{\vec u_2})\,\textcolor{#c792ea}{\vec u_2}$$

With \(\vec u_1 = [0.6, 0.8]\), \(\vec u_2 = [-0.8, 0.6]\) and \(\vec x = [3, 2]\): no equations to solve.

$$\vec x\cdot\vec u_1 = 1.8 + 1.6 = \textcolor{#f0a35e}{3.4},\qquad \vec x\cdot\vec u_2 = -2.4 + 1.2 = \textcolor{#f0a35e}{-1.2}$$

Check: \(3.4[0.6, 0.8] - 1.2[-0.8, 0.6] = [2.04 + 0.96,\ 2.72 - 0.72] = [3, 2]\).
