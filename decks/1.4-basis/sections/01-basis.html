Act 1 of 5

What a basis is

The fewest vectors that still reach every point.

Which sets are a basis?

\(\hat\imath\) and \(\hat\jmath\) work. So do two skewed vectors. Two parallel vectors fall short, and three are one too many.

Act 1 / Definition

A basis

$$\textcolor{#ffd166}{\text{basis}} = \textcolor{#6fd0c0}{\text{linearly independent}} \;+\; \textcolor{#f0a35e}{\text{spans the space}}$$

Spanning means every vector is reachable. Independence means none is wasted.

Spans only

\(\hat\imath, \hat\jmath, [1, 1]\) reach everything, but \([1, 1] = \hat\imath + \hat\jmath\) is redundant.

Independent only

\([2, 1]\) alone is independent, but it reaches only a line.

Same arrow, two bases

The arrow is fixed. Its coordinates are \((3, 2)\) on one grid and \((\tfrac53, \tfrac13)\) on the other.

Act 1 / Coordinates

Coordinates depend on the basis

$$\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = 3\textcolor{#6fd0c0}{\hat\imath} + 2\textcolor{#c792ea}{\hat\jmath} \qquad \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \tfrac53\textcolor{#6fd0c0}{\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}} + \tfrac13\textcolor{#c792ea}{\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}}$$

Check: \(\tfrac53[2,1] + \tfrac13[-1,1] = [\tfrac{10}{3} - \tfrac13,\ \tfrac53 + \tfrac13] = [3, 2]\).

Act 1 / The default

The standard basis in n dimensions

$$\vec e_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \end{bmatrix},\quad \vec e_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \end{bmatrix},\quad \dots,\quad \vec e_n = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

In the plane \(\vec e_1 = \hat\imath\) and \(\vec e_2 = \hat\jmath\). Writing a vector as a list of numbers silently picks this basis.
