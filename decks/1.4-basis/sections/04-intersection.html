Intersection of subspaces

What two subspaces share.

Two planes meet in a line

The intersection of subspaces is a subspace. A line missing the plane meets it only at the origin, \(\{\vec 0\}\).

Act 4 / Worked example

The planes \(z = 0\) and \(x = 0\)

$$\{z = 0\} = \operatorname{span}\Bigl\{\textcolor{#6fd0c0}{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}}, \textcolor{#c792ea}{\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}}\Bigr\} \qquad \{x = 0\} = \operatorname{span}\Bigl\{\textcolor{#c792ea}{\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}}, \textcolor{#8bd17c}{\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}}\Bigr\}$$

\(\{z = 0\}\) is the \(xy\)-plane and \(\{x = 0\}\) is the \(yz\)-plane.

A point is in both when \(z = 0\) and \(x = 0\), which leaves \([0, y, 0]\).

$$\{z = 0\} \cap \{x = 0\} = \operatorname{span}\Bigl\{\textcolor{#c792ea}{\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}}\Bigr\} = \textcolor{#f0a35e}{\text{the } y\text{-axis}}$$
Act 4 / A trap

The union is usually not a subspace

$$\textcolor{#6fd0c0}{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}} \in \{z = 0\},\quad \textcolor{#8bd17c}{\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}} \in \{x = 0\},\quad \textcolor{#ff6b6b}{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}} \notin \text{either}$$

The intersection keeps the add and scale rules. The union breaks closure under addition.
