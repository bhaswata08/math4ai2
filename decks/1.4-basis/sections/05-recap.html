Act 4 / Recap

Everything in this block

Basis

Independent and spanning. Every vector is one combination of it.

Dimension

The size of any basis: line 1, plane 2, space 3.

Coordinates

Same arrow, different numbers in a different basis.

Intersection

What both subspaces allow. Again a subspace.

Act 4 / Practice

By hand

  1. Is \(\left\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\right\}\) a basis of \(\mathbb{R}^2\)? What are the coordinates of \(\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\) in it? Yes: independent, and two vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^2\). \(\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = 1\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + 2\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\), so \((1, 2)\).
  2. Are \(\left\{\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 9 \\ 11 \end{bmatrix}\right\}\) linearly independent?
  3. Let \(X = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}\), \(Y = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\). Describe \(\operatorname{span}(X)\), \(\operatorname{span}(Y)\) and their intersection.
