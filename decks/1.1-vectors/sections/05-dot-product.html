The dot product

Two vectors in, one number out. That number says how much they agree.

30 min

Multiply matching entries, then add

\(\vec v \cdot \vec w = v_1 w_1 + v_2 w_2\). The answer is a single number, not a vector.

Any dimension, same rule

\(\vec v \cdot \vec w = \sum_i v_i w_i\). Both vectors must have the same number of entries.

The same number, as a picture

Project \(\vec w\) onto the line through \(\vec v\), then multiply the projection's length by the length of \(\vec v\).

Only the angle can make it negative

\(\vec v \cdot \vec w = \|\vec v\|\,\|\vec w\| \cos\theta\). Lengths are never negative, so the sign comes from \(\cos\theta\) alone.

Pointing apart gives a negative number

When the projection lands behind the origin, the product is negative.

The sign is an angle test

Positive: roughly the same direction. Zero: perpendicular. Negative: roughly opposite.

The dot product measures alignment

Obtuse angle: misaligned, negative. Right angle: perpendicular, zero. Acute angle: aligned, positive.

Order does not matter

\(\vec v \cdot \vec w = \vec w \cdot \vec v\). Projecting either one onto the other gives the same number.

Act 5 / Where this shows up

A neuron starts with a dot product

$$z = \textcolor{#c792ea}{\vec w} \cdot \textcolor{#6fd0c0}{\vec x}$$

Weights \(\vec w = [2, -1]\) meet inputs \(\vec x = [3, 4]\):

\(z = 2 \cdot 3 + (-1) \cdot 4 = \textcolor{#f0a35e}{2}\)

A large \(z\) means the input lines up with what the neuron's weights look for.

the full neuron adds a bias and an activation: \(\sigma(\vec w \cdot \vec x + b)\)

Act 5 / Where this shows up

A language model picks the next word with dot products

"I'm visiting Italy and want a classic pasta dish, so I ordered ____."

$$\text{score} = \textcolor{#6fd0c0}{\vec h} \cdot \textcolor{#c792ea}{\vec w} = \|\textcolor{#6fd0c0}{\vec h}\|\,\|\textcolor{#c792ea}{\vec w}\| \cos\theta$$

\(\vec h\): the model's state after the prompt, \(\|\vec h\| = 10\). \(\vec w\): one vector per word.

Spaghetti: \(10 \cdot 6 \cdot 0.95 = \textcolor{#f0a35e}{57}\)

Orecchiette points almost as well, but its short \(\vec w\) drops it below pizza.

illustrative values. The highest score becomes the most likely next word.
