Act 1 / Three views

One object, three readings

A list of numbers

\(\begin{bmatrix} \textcolor{#8bd17c}{3} \\ \textcolor{#e8798a}{-1} \end{bmatrix}\)
Computer science: order matters, and you can compute with it

An arrow

Physics: a length and a direction, free to sit anywhere

A data point

Data science: one row of a table, one point in a cloud

Linear algebra moves between these freely. The list is what the computer stores; the arrow is what you draw.

Act 1 / A list of numbers

Order and length are part of the vector

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \neq \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$\vec v \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$$

Same numbers, different order: a different vector.

\(n\) entries, each a real number. \(n\) is the dimension.

A dataset is a cloud of vectors

Every point is the tip of a vector from the origin. Keep only the tips and the shape of the data is what is left.

Act 1 / Where this shows up

A language model stores every word as a vector

Each word maps to a list of numbers, typically 768 or 1024 of them.

\(\text{cat} \mapsto \begin{bmatrix} 0.21 \\ -1.30 \\ 0.77 \\ \vdots \\ 0.05 \end{bmatrix}\)
