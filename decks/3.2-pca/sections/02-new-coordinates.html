Act 2 of 9

Data in a new basis

Every new coordinate is a dot product, so one matrix product rewrites the whole dataset.

15 min
Act 2 / Setup

The data matrix

$$X = \begin{bmatrix} \text{—}\ \mathbf x_1^T\ \text{—} \\ \vdots \\ \text{—}\ \mathbf x_m^T\ \text{—} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb R^{m \times n}$$
  • \(m\) data points (rows), \(n\) features (columns).
  • Centre every column: subtract its mean, so \(\sum_i \mathbf x_i = \mathbf 0\). This step is assumed from here on.
  • If the features are in different units (cm and km, rupees and years), also divide each column by its standard deviation. Otherwise the column with the largest numbers dominates.
  • New basis: \(P = [\,\mathbf p_1 \cdots \mathbf p_n\,]\) with orthonormal columns, so \(P^TP = I\).
Act 2 / Coordinates

Coordinates are dot products

Write a point in the new basis, then take the dot product of both sides with \(\mathbf p_k\):

$$\mathbf x_i = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \alpha_{ij}\,\mathbf p_j \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbf x_i^T\mathbf p_k = \sum_{j} \alpha_{ij}\,\underbrace{\mathbf p_j^T\mathbf p_k}_{1 \text{ if } j=k,\ 0 \text{ otherwise}} = \alpha_{ik}$$

  • All \(n\) coordinates of one point: \([\alpha_{i1} \cdots \alpha_{in}] = \mathbf x_i^T P\), a \(1 \times n\) row.
  • Stack the \(m\) rows:
$$\underset{m\times n}{\hat X} = \underset{m\times n}{X}\ \underset{n\times n}{P}$$

Each new column is a mix of centred columns, so \(\hat X\) is centred too.

One point, then all of them

Coordinate \(\alpha_{ij}\) is the length of the shadow of \(\mathbf x_i\) on \(\mathbf p_j\). Row \(i\) of \(XP\) holds all of them.

Act 2 / Covariance

The covariance matrix

$$C_{jk} = \frac1m \sum_{i=1}^{m} (x_{ij} - \mu_j)(x_{ik} - \mu_k) \;\overset{\boldsymbol\mu = \mathbf 0}{=}\; \frac1m\,(\text{column } j)\cdot(\text{column } k)$$

$$C = \tfrac1m X^TX \in \mathbb R^{n \times n}$$
  • Diagonal: \(C_{jj}\) is the variance of feature \(j\). Off-diagonal: covariance of features \(j\) and \(k\).
  • Symmetric: \(C^T = \tfrac1m X^T X = C\).
  • np.cov divides by \(m - 1\). Same eigenvectors, eigenvalues scaled by \(m/(m-1)\).

The sign of a covariance

Points in quadrants I and III add a positive product, II and IV a negative one. Here the positives win: \(C_{12} = 1.5\).

Act 2 / Example

Four points, by hand

$$X = \begin{bmatrix}2&2\\-2&-2\\1&-1\\-1&1\end{bmatrix}$$

Column sums are 0: already centred. \(m = 4\).

$$X^TX = \begin{bmatrix}4+4+1+1 & 4+4-1-1\\ \cdot & 4+4+1+1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}10&6\\6&10\end{bmatrix}$$

$$C = \tfrac14 X^TX = \begin{bmatrix}2.5&1.5\\1.5&2.5\end{bmatrix}$$

Both features have variance 2.5, and they move together (covariance 1.5). This example carries through the rest of the block.
