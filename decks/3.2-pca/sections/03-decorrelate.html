Interpretation 1: decorrelate

Ask for new coordinates with zero covariance, and the eigenvectors of \(C\) are the answer.

12 min
Act 3 / New covariance

Covariance in the new basis

\(\hat X = XP\) is centred, so its covariance matrix is

$$\hat C = \frac1m \hat X^T\hat X = \frac1m (XP)^T(XP) = P^T\Big(\frac1m X^TX\Big)P = P^T C\,P$$

  • \(\hat C_{jk}\) is the covariance between new coordinates \(j\) and \(k\).
  • Goal "no redundancy": \(\hat C_{jk} = 0\) for every \(j \ne k\).

We need an orthonormal \(P\) that makes \(P^TCP\) diagonal.

Act 3 / The answer

Diagonalise \(C\)

\(C\) is real and symmetric, so by the spectral theorem (Block 2.4)

$$C = Q\Lambda Q^T, \qquad Q^TQ = I, \qquad \Lambda = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_n)$$
  • Choose \(P = Q\): then \(P^TCP = Q^TQ\,\Lambda\,Q^TQ = \Lambda\). Every covariance is 0.
  • The principal directions are the eigenvectors of \(C\), which are also the eigenvectors of \(X^TX\).
  • \(\hat C_{jj} = \lambda_j\): the variance of new coordinate \(j\) is the \(j\)-th eigenvalue.

Rotate until the covariance vanishes

The off-diagonal entry of \(P^TCP\) changes as the basis turns. It reaches 0 exactly at the eigenvectors, where \(\hat C = \mathrm{diag}(4, 0.25)\).

Act 3 / Example

The four points, decorrelated

$$\det(C - \lambda I) = (2.5 - \lambda)^2 - 1.5^2 = 0$$

\(\lambda_1 = 4\), \(\mathbf p_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(1, 1)\)
\(\lambda_2 = 1\), \(\mathbf p_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(1, -1)\)

$$\hat X = XP = \begin{bmatrix}2\sqrt2&0\\-2\sqrt2&0\\0&\sqrt2\\0&-\sqrt2\end{bmatrix}$$

$$\hat C = \tfrac14\hat X^T\hat X = \begin{bmatrix}4&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$$

In the new coordinates the two features are uncorrelated, and the first holds \(4/5 = 80\%\) of the variance.

Act 3 / Open question

Which directions do we keep?

  • Interpretation 1 fixes the basis, but it treats every eigenvector the same.
  • We still have to choose which \(k\) of the \(n\) directions to keep. Intuition says keep the large \(\lambda_j\). Act 4 shows why.
