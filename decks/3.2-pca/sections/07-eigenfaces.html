Eigenfaces

A face image is a point in \(\mathbb R^{4096}\), and about fifty directions are enough to describe it.

Act 7 / Setup

A face is a vector

  • A \(64 \times 64\) grey image, read row by row, is a vector \(\mathbf x_i \in \mathbb R^{4096}\).
  • 400 images of 40 people: \(X\) is \(400 \times 4096\). Subtract the mean face \(\boldsymbol\mu\).
  • Principal directions \(\mathbf p_j \in \mathbb R^{4096}\) come from the SVD, since \(n \gg m\).
  • Reshape each \(\mathbf p_j\) back to \(64 \times 64\) and look at it.

The principal directions look like faces

The mean face and the leading eigenvectors of \(C\), each reshaped to an image. Early ones capture lighting and overall shape, later ones finer detail.

Rebuilding one face

\(\hat{\mathbf x} = \boldsymbol\mu + \sum_{j\le k}\alpha_j\mathbf p_j\) with \(\alpha_j = (\mathbf x - \boldsymbol\mu)^T\mathbf p_j\). \(k\) numbers replace 4096.

Act 7 / Uses and limits

Where PCA is used, and where it fails

Compress features

Fewer, uncorrelated inputs for a downstream model. Fewer parameters, less overfitting.

Visualise

Project 768-dimensional embeddings onto \(\mathbf p_1, \mathbf p_2\) to look at clusters.

Denoise, whiten

Drop the small-\(\lambda\) directions. Divide each score by \(\sqrt{\lambda_j}\) to get unit variance in every direction.

Limits

Only linear subspaces. Variance is not the same as usefulness for a label. Sensitive to feature scale and to outliers.
