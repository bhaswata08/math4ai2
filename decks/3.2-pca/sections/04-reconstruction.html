Act 4 of 9

Lose the least, keep the most

Interpretations 2 and 3: the smallest reconstruction error and the largest variance turn out to be the same goal.

18 min
Act 4 / The question

Keep one direction. Which one?

  • Keep only the coordinate along a unit vector \(\mathbf p\): each point \(\mathbf x_i\) becomes its shadow \((\mathbf x_i^T\mathbf p)\,\mathbf p\).
  • Interpretation 2: choose \(\mathbf p\) so the shadows are as close as possible to the points (small reconstruction error).
  • Interpretation 3: choose \(\mathbf p\) so the shadows are as spread out as possible (large variance).

These look like two different goals. Pythagoras shows they are the same one.

Act 4 / Pythagoras

Kept plus lost is fixed

The shadow and the leftover are at a right angle, so for every point

$$\|\mathbf x_i\|^2 = \underbrace{(\mathbf x_i^T\mathbf p)^2}_{\text{kept}} + \underbrace{\|\mathbf x_i - (\mathbf x_i^T\mathbf p)\,\mathbf p\|^2}_{\text{lost}}$$
  • Add over all points: the left side depends only on the data, not on \(\mathbf p\).
  • So making the lost part small is the same as making the kept part large.

Captured plus lost is constant

As \(\mathbf p\) turns, the captured variance and the lost squared distances trade off, but always add to \(4.25\). The best direction is \(\mathbf p_1\).

Act 4 / Variance along \(\mathbf p\)

The kept variance is \(\mathbf p^TC\mathbf p\)

$$\frac1m\sum_{i}(\mathbf x_i^T\mathbf p)^2 = \frac1m\|X\mathbf p\|^2 = \mathbf p^T\Big(\frac1m X^TX\Big)\mathbf p = \mathbf p^TC\,\mathbf p$$

  • If \(\mathbf p\) is an eigenvector, \(C\mathbf p = \lambda\mathbf p\), so the kept variance is exactly \(\lambda\).
  • Each eigenvalue is the variance of the data along its eigenvector.
Act 4 / Why the top eigenvector

Any direction is a blend

Write \(\mathbf p\) in the eigenvector basis: \(\mathbf p = c_1\mathbf p_1 + c_2\mathbf p_2\), with \(c_1^2 + c_2^2 = 1\). Then

$$\mathbf p^TC\mathbf p = \lambda_1 c_1^2 + \lambda_2 c_2^2$$
  • This is a weighted average of \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\). It is largest when all the weight is on \(\lambda_1\), that is, at \(\mathbf p = \mathbf p_1\).
  • Our data: any direction keeps between \(0.25\) and \(4\). \(\mathbf p_1\) keeps \(4\) and loses \(0.25\).
  • The same holds in \(n\) dimensions. To keep \(k\) directions, take the eigenvectors of the \(k\) largest eigenvalues. The loss is the sum of the eigenvalues you drop.

Keep one direction

Drop \(\mathbf p_2\): each point becomes one number \(\alpha_{i1}\). The column \(X\mathbf p_1\) keeps \(4/4.25 = 94\%\) of the variance.
