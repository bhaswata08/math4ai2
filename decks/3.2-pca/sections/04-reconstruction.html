Lose the least, keep the most

Interpretation 2: drop a direction and measure what you lose. Interpretation 3: keep the most spread. They pick the same directions.

Act 4 / The setup

Keep two coordinates out of three

Data in 3D, written in the principal directions:

$$\mathbf x_i = \alpha_{i1}\mathbf p_1 + \alpha_{i2}\mathbf p_2 + \alpha_{i3}\mathbf p_3$$
  • Store only two numbers per point, \(\alpha_{i1}, \alpha_{i2}\). Rebuild the point from them: \(\hat{\mathbf x}_i = \alpha_{i1}\mathbf p_1 + \alpha_{i2}\mathbf p_2\).
  • The rebuilt point misses by \(\mathbf x_i - \hat{\mathbf x}_i\). The reconstruction loss is the average squared miss: \(\tfrac1m\sum_i\|\mathbf x_i - \hat{\mathbf x}_i\|^2\).

Which direction should we ditch so the loss is smallest?

Ditch \(\mathbf p_3\), see what is lost

Every point drops straight onto the \(\mathbf p_1\)–\(\mathbf p_2\) plane. The red drop is \(\mathbf x_i - \hat{\mathbf x}_i\), what we lose. Its average square is \(0.5 = \lambda_3\).

Act 4 / The loss

Why the loss is \(\lambda_3\)

  • The miss is the part we dropped: \(\mathbf x_i - \hat{\mathbf x}_i = \alpha_{i3}\mathbf p_3\).
  • \(\mathbf p_3\) has length 1, so the squared miss is \(\alpha_{i3}^2\).
  • Average over the points: \(\tfrac1m\sum_i \alpha_{i3}^2\) is the variance of the third coordinate, and Act 3 showed that is \(\lambda_3\).

Ditch a principal direction and you lose exactly its eigenvalue.

Ditch each one in turn

Ditch \(\mathbf p_1\): lose 4.0. Ditch \(\mathbf p_2\): lose 1.5. Ditch \(\mathbf p_3\): lose 0.5. Ditch the direction with the smallest eigenvalue.

Act 4 / Only eigenvectors?

Could another direction lose less?

  • We only tried ditching the eigenvectors. A tilted direction in between might do better.
  • It cannot. The best direction is always the top eigenvector, and to keep \(k\) directions you take the eigenvectors of the \(k\) largest eigenvalues. The loss is the sum of the eigenvalues you drop.

Keep one direction

The 40-point cloud: drop \(\mathbf p_2\), and each point becomes one number \(\alpha_{i1}\). The column \(X\mathbf p_1\) keeps \(4/4.25 = 94\%\) of the variance.
