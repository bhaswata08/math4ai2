Slice along \(x\)

For \(f(x,y) = x^2 y + \sin y\) at \((1, 0.5)\), freeze \(y\). The surface becomes a curve in \(x\), and its slope is \(\partial f/\partial x\).

Slice along \(y\)

Same \(f(x,y) = x^2 y + \sin y\) at \((1, 0.5)\); freeze \(x\) instead. Two slopes, one per input. Stacked together they form the gradient, next block.

Act 5 / How to compute

Treat the other inputs as constants

$$f(x, y) = x^2 y + \sin y$$

At \((1, 0.5)\): \(\partial f/\partial x = 1\) and \(\partial f/\partial y = 1 + \cos 0.5 \approx 1.88\), the two slopes in the clips.

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 2xy$$

\(y\) is a constant, \(\sin y\) disappears

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = x^2 + \cos y$$

\(x^2\) is a constant coefficient
