Act 7 of 9

Positive definite

Symmetric matrices that curve upward in every direction.

A matrix draws a surface

The height is \(z = x^TAx\). A bowl, a trough, a saddle.

Act 7 / Definition

Three kinds of symmetric matrix

NameConditionSurfaceExample
Positive definite (PD)\(x^TAx>0\) for all \(x\neq 0\)bowl\(\mathrm{diag}(2,1)\)
Positive semi-definite (PSD)\(x^TAx\ge 0\) for all \(x\)trough\(\mathrm{diag}(1,0)\)
Indefiniteboth signs occursaddle\(\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)\)

For \(\mathrm{diag}(2,1)\): \(x^TAx = 2x^2 + y^2\), which is zero only at the origin.

The angle test

\(x^TAx = \|x\|\,\|Ax\|\cos\theta\). Positive definite means \(Ax\) never turns more than 90 degrees from \(x\).

Act 7 / Testing

A quick test for a symmetric 2×2

$$A=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{bmatrix}\ \text{is PD}\iff a>0\ \text{and}\ ac-b^2>0$$

Complete the square (\(a\neq0\)):

$$x^TAx=ax_1^2+2bx_1x_2+cx_2^2=a\Big(x_1+\tfrac{b}{a}x_2\Big)^2+\tfrac{ac-b^2}{a}\,x_2^2$$

Both squares are positive for every \(x\neq0\) exactly when \(a>0\) and \(ac-b^2>0\).

Check: \(\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}\) has \(a=2>0\) and \(ac-b^2=3>0\), so it is PD. \(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}\) has \(\det=-1\), so it is not.

The test applies to symmetric matrices only.

Level curves tell the same story

The curve \(x^TAx=1\): a closed ellipse when \(ac-b^2>0\) and \(a>0\), two branches when \(ac-b^2<0\), two parallel lines when \(ac-b^2=0\).

Act 7 / Always PSD

\(X^TX\) is never negative

$$x^T(X^TX)x=(Xx)^T(Xx)=\|Xx\|^2\ \ge 0$$

Covariance

Built as \(X^TX\) (centred data), so it is PSD. A variance cannot be negative.

Hessian (Day 4)

At a strict minimum the Hessian is positive definite: the loss curves up in every direction.
