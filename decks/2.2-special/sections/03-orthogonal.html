Orthogonal matrices

Matrices that move things without stretching them.

Perpendicular means dot product zero

\(\begin{bmatrix}1\\3\end{bmatrix}\cdot\begin{bmatrix}6\\-2\end{bmatrix}=6-6=0\). Look for the right-angle mark.

Act 3 / Definition

Orthonormal columns

\(Q\) is orthogonal when its columns are unit vectors at right angles to each other.

$$Q^TQ = I \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad Q^{-1}=Q^T$$

\(Q^{-1}\) is the matrix that undoes \(Q\). Act 5 treats inverses in general.

Entry \((i,j)\) of \(Q^TQ\) is column \(i\) dotted with column \(j\): 1 on the diagonal, 0 elsewhere.

To undo an orthogonal matrix, transpose it. No elimination needed.

A pure rotation

\(\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\-1&1\end{bmatrix}\) turns the rectangle clockwise by 45 degrees. Its size and shape stay the same.

The transpose undoes it

Entry \((i,j)\) of \(Q^TQ\) is \(q_i\cdot q_j\): 1 on the diagonal, 0 off it. So \(Q^T\) turns the square back: \(Q^{-1}=Q^T\).

The same undo in 3D

\(Q\) turns the cube 25° about the \(z\)-axis. \(Q^T\) turns it −25°, back where it started: \(Q^TQ = I\).

Act 3 / What it keeps

Lengths and angles survive

$$\|Qx\|^2=(Qx)^T(Qx)=x^TQ^TQx=x^Tx=\|x\|^2$$

Dot products survive the same way, so angles are kept too:

$$(Qx)\cdot(Qy)=x^TQ^TQy=x^Ty$$
Act 3 / In AI

Why deep nets like orthogonal matrices

Weight initialisation

An orthogonal weight matrix keeps the norm of the signal. Stacked layers neither blow it up nor shrink it to zero.

Rotary position embeddings

Each pair of coordinates is rotated by an angle that depends on the token position. Rotations are orthogonal, so vector lengths are untouched.
