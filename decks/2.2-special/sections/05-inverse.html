Act 5 of 9

The inverse

Run the map backwards. It works only when nothing was lost.

Apply, then undo

\(A = \begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}\) bends the grid. \(A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\-1&2\end{bmatrix}\) straightens it again.

Scale up, scale down

\(A = 0.5I\) halves the picture. \(A^{-1} = 2I\) doubles it back, so \(A^{-1}A = I\).

Act 5 / Definition

The inverse matrix

$$A^{-1}A = AA^{-1} = I$$

Doing \(A\) and then \(A^{-1}\) is the same as doing nothing.

\(A^{-1}\) exists exactly when \(A\) squashes nothing: no direction is sent to \(\mathbf{0}\).

No way back

\(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\) sends many inputs to one output. Nothing can undo that.

Squashing onto a line or plane

The cube is flattened to a plane, a line and a point. None of these maps has an inverse.

Act 5 / Singular

When there is no inverse

Nothing squashed

Invertible. Nothing is lost, every output has one input.

Something squashed

Singular. Space is flattened, and the lost information is gone.

Act 5 / Product

Socks and shoes

$$(AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1}$$

Put on socks (\(B\)), then shoes (\(A\)). To undo, take off the shoes first, then the socks.

Check: \((AB)(B^{-1}A^{-1}) = A(BB^{-1})A^{-1} = AIA^{-1} = I\).
