How the special matrices fit together

Square matrices \(n\times n\)
Symmetric \(A=A^T\)
Positive semi-definite \(x^TAx\ge0\)
Invertible \(A^{-1}\) exists
Orthogonal\(Q^{-1}=Q^T\)
Positive definite\(x^TAx>0\)
Orthogonal projectionsquash onto a line
Diagonalscale each axis

Positive definite sits where Invertible overlaps Symmetric: it is symmetric, positive semi-definite and invertible. The identity \(I\) is diagonal, orthogonal and positive definite. Reflections such as \(\mathrm{diag}(-1,1)\) are orthogonal and symmetric, so those two boxes also overlap.

Act 9 / Not PSD

Orthogonal, but not positive semi-definite

Dashed: the unit square. Yellow: where \(A\) sends it. Arrows: where \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) land.

Swap the axes (mirror in the dotted line)
\(\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}\)
\(x^TAx=2x_1x_2\)
both signs: indefinite
Rotate by 180°
\(\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}\)
\(x^TAx=-x_1^2-x_2^2\)
always negative: negative definite
Rotate by 90°
\(\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}\)
\(x^TAx=0\)
not symmetric, so not PSD

Among the orthogonal matrices, only \(I\) is positive semi-definite.

Act 9 / Not PSD

Diagonal, but not positive semi-definite

Dashed: the unit square. Yellow: where \(A\) sends it. Arrows: where \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) land.

Flip one axis
\(\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}\)
\(x^TAx=x_1^2-x_2^2\)
both signs: indefinite
Flip and stretch both
\(\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\0&-2\end{bmatrix}\)
\(x^TAx=-x_1^2-2x_2^2\)
always negative: negative definite
Squash one, flip the other
\(\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\0&-2\end{bmatrix}\)
\(x^TAx=-2x_2^2\)
never positive: negative semi-definite

A diagonal matrix is positive semi-definite exactly when every diagonal entry is \(\ge0\).
