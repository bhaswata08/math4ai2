Act 3 of 5

Minima, maxima, saddles

Where the gradient vanishes, the Hessian decides what you are standing on.

Three flat points

All three have \(\nabla f = 0\). Only the eigenvalues of \(H\) tell them apart.

One function, two flat points

\(\partial f/\partial y = 2x - 2y = 0\) gives \(y = x\); \(\partial f/\partial x = x(3x+2) = 0\) gives \(x = 0\) or \(-\tfrac23\). At \((0,0)\): \(\lambda = -1 \pm \sqrt5\) (\(1.24, -3.24\)), mixed signs, saddle. At \((-\tfrac23,-\tfrac23)\): \(\lambda = -3 \pm \sqrt5\) (\(-0.76, -5.24\)), both negative, maximum.

Act 3 / Worked example

Solve \(\nabla f = \mathbf 0\)

$$f = x^3 + 2xy - y^2 \qquad \nabla f = \begin{bmatrix} 3x^2 + 2y \\ 2x - 2y \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf 0$$
  1. \(2x - 2y = 0 \Rightarrow y = x\).
  2. \(3x^2 + 2x = x(3x + 2) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 0\) or \(x = -\tfrac23\).
  3. \(H(0,0) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}\), \(\lambda = -1 \pm \sqrt5\) (\(1.24, -3.24\)): saddle.
  4. \(H(-\tfrac23,-\tfrac23) = \begin{bmatrix} -4 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}\), \(\lambda = -3 \pm \sqrt5\) (\(-0.76, -5.24\)): maximum.
Act 3 / The second-derivative test

At a point where \(\nabla f = \mathbf 0\)

Minimum

All \(\lambda_i > 0\): \(H\) is positive definite.

Maximum

All \(\lambda_i < 0\): \(H\) is negative definite.

Saddle

Mixed signs: up in some directions, down in others.

Undecided

Some \(\lambda_i = 0\): the test is silent. Look at higher-order terms.

In a model with millions of parameters, all-positive is rare: most flat points of a loss are saddles.
