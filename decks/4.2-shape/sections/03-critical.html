Act 3 of 5

Minima, maxima, saddles

Where the gradient vanishes, the Hessian decides what you are standing on.

Three flat points

All three have \(\nabla f = 0\). Only the eigenvalues of \(H\) tell them apart.

One function, two flat points

\(\partial f/\partial y = 2x - 2y = 0\) gives \(y = x\); \(\partial f/\partial x = x(3x+2) = 0\) gives \(x = 0\) or \(-\tfrac23\). At \((0,0)\): \(\lambda = -1 \pm \sqrt5\) (\(1.24, -3.24\)), mixed signs, saddle. At \((-\tfrac23,-\tfrac23)\): \(\lambda = -3 \pm \sqrt5\) (\(-0.76, -5.24\)), both negative, maximum.

Act 3 / Worked example, step 1

Take the two partials

$$f(x, y) = x^3 + 2xy - y^2$$

Differentiate in \(x\) with \(y\) held constant, then in \(y\) with \(x\) held constant. The gradient stacks the two:

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 3x^2 + 2y \qquad \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 2x - 2y$$
$$\nabla f = \begin{bmatrix} 3x^2 + 2y \\ 2x - 2y \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf 0$$

A critical point is any \((x,y)\) where both entries vanish at once. That is two equations in two unknowns.

Act 3 / Worked example, step 2

Solve the system

  1. The second row is linear: \(2x - 2y = 0 \Rightarrow y = x\).
  2. Put \(y = x\) into the first row: \(3x^2 + 2x = x(3x + 2) = 0\).
  3. \(x = 0\), so \(y = 0\): the point \((0, 0)\).
  4. \(3x + 2 = 0\), so \(x = y = -\tfrac23\): the point \(\left(-\tfrac23, -\tfrac23\right)\).

Two flat points. The gradient says nothing about which is which.

Act 3 / Worked example, step 3

Build the Hessian

Differentiate each entry of \(\nabla f\) again, in \(x\) and in \(y\):

$$f_{xx} = 6x \qquad f_{xy} = f_{yx} = 2 \qquad f_{yy} = -2$$
$$H(x, y) = \begin{bmatrix} 6x & 2 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$$

Only \(x\) survives in \(H\), so the two critical points get different matrices. The off-diagonals agree, as they must.

Act 3 / Worked example, step 4

Classify each point by its eigenvalues

At \((0, 0)\)

$$H = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$\det(H - \lambda I) = \lambda^2 + 2\lambda - 4 = 0$$
$$\lambda = -1 \pm \sqrt5 \approx 1.24,\; -3.24$$

Opposite signs: up along one eigenvector, down along the other. A saddle.

At \(\left(-\tfrac23, -\tfrac23\right)\)

$$H = \begin{bmatrix} -4 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$\det(H - \lambda I) = \lambda^2 + 6\lambda + 4 = 0$$
$$\lambda = -3 \pm \sqrt5 \approx -0.76,\; -5.24$$

Both negative: \(H\) is negative definite, the surface curves down every way. A local maximum.

Act 3 / The second-derivative test

At a point where \(\nabla f = \mathbf 0\)

Minimum

All \(\lambda_i > 0\): \(H\) is positive definite.

Maximum

All \(\lambda_i < 0\): \(H\) is negative definite.

Saddle

Mixed signs: up in some directions, down in others.

Undecided

Some \(\lambda_i = 0\): the test is silent. Look at higher-order terms.

In a model with millions of parameters, all-positive is rare: most flat points of a loss are saddles.
