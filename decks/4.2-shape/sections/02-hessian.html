Act 2 / One variable to many

Second-order approximation

$$f(a + h) \approx f(a) + f'(a)\,h + \tfrac12\,f''(a)\,h^2$$
$$f(\mathbf p + \mathbf h) \approx f(\mathbf p) + \nabla f^{\top}\mathbf h + \tfrac12\,\mathbf h^{\top} H\,\mathbf h$$
$$H = \begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1^2} & \cdots & \dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_1 \partial x_n} \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_n \partial x_1} & \cdots & \dfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_n^2} \end{bmatrix}$$

For smooth \(f\) the mixed partials agree, so \(H\) is symmetric: real eigenvalues, orthogonal eigenvectors.

Plane, then bowl

The gradient gives the tangent plane. The Hessian bends it into the best-fitting quadratic.

Act 2 / Worked example

\(f(x, y) = x^3 + 2xy - y^2\)

$$\nabla f = \begin{bmatrix} 3x^2 + 2y \\ 2x - 2y \end{bmatrix}$$
$$H = \begin{bmatrix} 6x & 2 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$$

At \((1, 1)\): \(H = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}\). The two off-diagonal entries are equal, as promised.

Reading a Hessian

Along each eigenvector the function curves by the eigenvalue: up if positive, down if negative.
