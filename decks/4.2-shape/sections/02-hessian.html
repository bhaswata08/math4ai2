The Hessian

The same Taylor step, with a vector for the slope and a matrix for the curvature.

Act 2 / One variable to many

Second-order approximation

Take \(\mathbf p \in \mathbb R^n\) as the base point and \(\mathbf h\) as a small step away from it. \(\nabla f(\mathbf p)\) is the gradient there, and \(H(\mathbf p)\) is the Hessian: the matrix of second derivatives, \(H_{ij} = \tfrac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x_i \partial x_j}\) evaluated at \(\mathbf p\). In one variable:

$$f(a + h) \approx f(a) + f'(a)\,h + \tfrac12\,f''(a)\,h^2$$
$$f(\mathbf p + \mathbf h) \approx f(\mathbf p) + \nabla f(\mathbf p)^{\top}\mathbf h + \tfrac12\,\mathbf h^{\top} H(\mathbf p)\,\mathbf h$$

For smooth \(f\) the mixed partials agree, so \(H\) is symmetric: real eigenvalues, orthogonal eigenvectors.

From slices to a quadratic

Take \(f(x, y) = x^3 + 2xy - y^2\) at \(\mathbf p = (1,1)\): slope 5 along \(x\), none along \(y\). The gradient gives the tangent plane, the Hessian bends it.

Act 2 / Worked example

\(f(x, y) = x^3 + 2xy - y^2\) at \(\mathbf p = (1,1)\)

$$\nabla f = \begin{bmatrix} 3x^2 + 2y \\ 2x - 2y \end{bmatrix}$$
$$H = \begin{bmatrix} 6x & 2 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$\nabla f(1,1) = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
$$H(1,1) = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$$

At \(\mathbf p = (1, 1)\), \(f(\mathbf p) = 2\), so \(f(\mathbf p + \mathbf h) \approx 2 + 5h_1 + 3h_1^2 + 2h_1h_2 - h_2^2\). The two off-diagonal entries of \(H\) are equal, as promised.

Curvature in every direction

For \(\mathbf u = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)\): \(\mathbf u^{\top}H\mathbf u = 2\cos\theta\sin\theta = \sin 2\theta\). Max \(1\) at \(\theta = \pi/4\), min \(-1\) at \(3\pi/4\): the largest and smallest eigenvalues of \(H\).
