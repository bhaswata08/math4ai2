Rotate, stretch, rotate

Read \(U\Sigma V^T\) from right to left and watch each factor move space.

The unit circle becomes an ellipse

  • \(A=\begin{bmatrix}3&0\\4&5\end{bmatrix}\).
  • \(V^T\) turns \(\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2\) onto the axes.
  • \(\Sigma\) stretches by \(6.71\) and \(2.24\).
  • \(U\) turns the axes onto \(\mathbf{u}_1,\mathbf{u}_2\).

Perpendicular in, perpendicular out

  • \(A\mathbf{v}_i=\sigma_i\mathbf{u}_i\).
  • The \(\mathbf{v}_i\) are perpendicular, and so are the \(\mathbf{u}_i\).
  • This is the one perpendicular pair that \(A\) keeps perpendicular.
Act 4 / Reading the picture

The ellipse holds the whole SVD

  • Its axes point along \(\mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2\).
  • Its semi-axis lengths are \(\sigma_1, \sigma_2\).
  • The points of the circle that land on the axes are \(\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2\).

\(\sigma_1\) is the most \(A\) can stretch a unit vector, \(\sigma_2\) the least.

Act 4 / 3D to 2D

Example: a \(2 \times 3\) matrix

\(A = \begin{bmatrix}3&2&2\\2&3&-2\end{bmatrix} \qquad AA^T = \begin{bmatrix}17&8\\8&17\end{bmatrix}\)

The characteristic equation is \((17-\lambda)^2 - 64 = 0\), so \(\lambda = 25,\ 9\).

\((AA^T - \lambda I)\mathbf{u} = 0\) gives \(\mathbf{u}_1 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(1,1)\) for 25 and \(\mathbf{u}_2 = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(1,-1)\) for 9. \(U\) rotates by 45 degrees.

\(\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i} = 5,\ 3\), so \(\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\0&3&0\end{bmatrix}\). The \(\mathbf{v}_i = A^T\mathbf{u}_i/\sigma_i\) are the first two rows of the \(3 \times 3\) rotation \(V^T\).

\(\def\arraystretch{1.6}\begin{bmatrix}3&2&2\\2&3&-2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\0&3&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&0\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&-\frac{4}{\sqrt{18}}\\\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{1}{3}\end{bmatrix}\)

What \(A\) does in one go

\(\def\arraystretch{1.6}\begin{bmatrix}3&2&2\\2&3&-2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\0&3&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&0\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&-\frac{4}{\sqrt{18}}\\\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{1}{3}\end{bmatrix}\)
  • Apply \(A\) in one go to the unit cube.
  • It lands in the plane as a slanted hexagon.
  • Hard to read. The SVD splits it into three simple moves.

Step 1: rotate with \(V^T\)

\(\def\arraystretch{1.6}\begin{bmatrix}3&2&2\\2&3&-2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\0&3&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&0\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&-\frac{4}{\sqrt{18}}\\\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{1}{3}\end{bmatrix}\)
  • Step 1: \(V^T\) rotates in 3D.
  • \(\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2,\mathbf{v}_3\) land on the \(x\), \(y\), \(z\) axes.
  • Lengths and angles are unchanged.

Step 2: erase \(z\), stretch with \(\Sigma\)

\(\def\arraystretch{1.6}\begin{bmatrix}3&2&2\\2&3&-2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\0&3&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&0\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&-\frac{4}{\sqrt{18}}\\\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{1}{3}\end{bmatrix}\)
  • Step 2: \(\Sigma\) drops the third axis.
  • Then it stretches \(x\) by 5 and \(y\) by 3.
  • This is the only step that changes shape.

Step 3: rotate with \(U\)

\(\def\arraystretch{1.6}\begin{bmatrix}3&2&2\\2&3&-2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\0&3&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&0\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&-\frac{4}{\sqrt{18}}\\\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{1}{3}\end{bmatrix}\)
  • Step 3: \(U\) rotates in the plane.
  • The axes land on \(\mathbf{u}_1,\mathbf{u}_2\).
  • The result is exactly what \(A\) did in one go.

Three steps against one

\(\def\arraystretch{1.6}\begin{bmatrix}3&2&2\\2&3&-2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\0&3&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&0\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&-\frac{4}{\sqrt{18}}\\\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{1}{3}\end{bmatrix}\)
  • Left: \(A\) applied directly.
  • Right: \(V^T\), then \(\Sigma\), then \(U\).
  • The two shapes match.

The same steps on a sphere

\(\def\arraystretch{1.6}\begin{bmatrix}3&2&2\\2&3&-2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\0&3&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&0\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&-\frac{4}{\sqrt{18}}\\\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{1}{3}\end{bmatrix}\)
  • Same three steps, now on a sphere.
  • Step 1: \(V^T\) turns the sphere.
  • A sphere looks the same after a rotation, but the right singular vectors now lie on the axes.

The sphere becomes an ellipse

\(\def\arraystretch{1.6}\begin{bmatrix}3&2&2\\2&3&-2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\0&3&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&0\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&-\frac{4}{\sqrt{18}}\\\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{1}{3}\end{bmatrix}\)
  • Seen from above, the flattened sphere is a disc.
  • \(\Sigma\) stretches it to semi-axes 5 and 3.
  • The disc becomes an ellipse.

Rotate the ellipse into place

\(\def\arraystretch{1.6}\begin{bmatrix}3&2&2\\2&3&-2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\0&3&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&0\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&-\frac{4}{\sqrt{18}}\\\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{1}{3}\end{bmatrix}\)
  • Step 3: \(U\) turns the ellipse axes onto \(\mathbf{u}_1,\mathbf{u}_2\).
  • A unit sphere always lands on an ellipse with semi-axes \(\sigma_i\).
  • The singular values are the lengths of those axes.

The whole map in 3D

\(\def\arraystretch{1.6}\begin{bmatrix}3&2&2\\2&3&-2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&-\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\\\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5&0&0\\0&3&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&\frac{1}{\sqrt2}&0\\-\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{18}}&-\frac{4}{\sqrt{18}}\\\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{2}{3}&-\frac{1}{3}\end{bmatrix}\)
  • The whole map in one replay.
  • Unit sphere in \(\mathbb{R}^3\), flattened.
  • Ellipse with semi-axes \(\sigma_1=5\), \(\sigma_2=3\).
Act 4 / 2D to 3D

Example: a \(3 \times 2\) matrix

\(A = \begin{bmatrix}2&1\\3&1\\0&1.8\end{bmatrix}\)

\(A^TA = \begin{bmatrix}13&5\\5&5.24\end{bmatrix}\): trace 18.24, determinant 43.12, so \(\lambda^2 - 18.24\lambda + 43.12 = 0\) and \(\lambda \approx 15.45,\ 2.79\).

\(\sigma_1 = 3.93\), \(\sigma_2 = 1.67\). \(\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix}3.93&0\\0&1.67\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\) is a dimension adder with stretches.

What \(A\) does in one go

  • Now a \(3\times2\) matrix: 2D in, 3D out.
  • Flat discs in the plane land on a tilted plane in 3D.
  • Again hard to read in one step.

Step 1: rotate with \(V^T\)

  • Step 1: a small 2D rotation by \(V^T\), about 26 degrees.
  • \(\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2\) land on the axes.

Step 2: stretch, then add a dimension

  • Step 2: \(\Sigma\) stretches \(x\) by 3.93 and \(y\) by 1.67.
  • Then it lays the result flat in 3D: the dimension adder.

Step 3: rotate with \(U\) in 3D

  • Step 3: \(U\) is now a 3D rotation.
  • It tilts the flat shape onto the plane spanned by \(\mathbf{u}_1,\mathbf{u}_2\).

Four steps, any matrix

  • Rotate by \(V^T\).
  • Stretch by the \(\sigma_i\).
  • Erase or add dimensions.
  • Rotate by \(U\). This works for any matrix.
