Act 1 / Recap

The spectral decomposition

A real symmetric matrix \(S\) factors as

\(S = Q\,\Lambda\,Q^{T}\)

The columns of \(Q\) are the eigenvectors. For a symmetric matrix they are orthogonal, so \(Q^T = Q^{-1}\).

  • \(Q^T\) rotates the eigenvectors onto the axes
  • \(\Lambda\) stretches each axis by its eigenvalue
  • \(Q\) rotates back

The catch: \(S\) must be square and symmetric.

Act 1 / The catch

Most matrices are neither

  • A weight matrix of shape \(512 \times 768\) is not square.
  • A data matrix with 10,000 rows and 20 columns is not square.
  • \(\begin{bmatrix}3&0\\4&5\end{bmatrix}\) is square but not symmetric. Its eigenvectors are not perpendicular.

We want a rotate, stretch, rotate picture that works for all of them.

Any shape

  • Eigen-decomposition needed a square, symmetric matrix.
  • The SVD has no such condition: square, wide or tall, any rank.
  • That is why it is the workhorse for real data matrices.

\(A = U\,\Sigma\,V^T\)

  • Read right to left, as the matrix acts on a vector: first \(V^T\), then \(\Sigma\), then \(U\).
  • Two different rotations, one on each side. The eigen case used \(Q\) and \(Q^T\).
  • The stretch \(\Sigma\) sits between them.
