Act 1 / Recap

The spectral decomposition

A real symmetric matrix \(S\) factors as

\(S = Q\,\Lambda\,Q^{T}\)
  • \(Q^T\) rotates the eigenvectors onto the axes
  • \(\Lambda\) stretches each axis by its eigenvalue
  • \(Q\) rotates back

The catch: \(S\) must be square and symmetric.

Act 1 / The catch

Most matrices are neither

  • A weight matrix of shape \(512 \times 768\) is not square.
  • A data matrix with 10,000 rows and 20 columns is not square.
  • \(\begin{bmatrix}3&0\\4&5\end{bmatrix}\) is square but not symmetric. Its eigenvectors are not perpendicular.

We want a rotate, stretch, rotate picture that works for all of them.

Any shape

Square, wide, tall: the SVD puts no condition on symmetry, dimension or rank.

\(A = U\,\Sigma\,V^T\)

Two different rotations, \(V^T\) and \(U\), with a stretch \(\Sigma\) between them.
