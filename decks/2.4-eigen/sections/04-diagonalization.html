Diagonalization

Use the eigenvectors as the axes. In that basis the matrix is just scaling.

Act 4 / Statement

\(A = PDP^{-1}\)

  • \(P\): eigenvectors as columns
  • \(D\): eigenvalues on the diagonal, in the same order
  • Possible when \(A\) has \(n\) independent eigenvectors

Read right to left: go to the eigenbasis, scale, come back.

Change basis, stretch, change back

Follow \(\mathbf{x}=(3,0)\): \(P^{-1}\) gives its eigenbasis coordinates \((2,1)\), \(D\) stretches them to \((10,2)\), \(P\) returns \((12,6)=A\mathbf{x}\).

Act 4 / Worked example

Our matrix, factored

$$\large\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\2&3\end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&-2\end{bmatrix}}_{P}\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix}5&0\\0&2\end{bmatrix}}_{D}\underbrace{\tfrac13\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&-1\end{bmatrix}}_{P^{-1}}$$

Multiply it out once to see that it returns \(A\).

Act 4 / Powers

\(A^k = PD^kP^{-1}\)

Write \(A^2\) out and cancel the middle pair:

$$A^2 = (PDP^{-1})(PDP^{-1}) = PD(P^{-1}P)DP^{-1} = PD^2P^{-1}$$

Each new copy of \(A\) adds one more \(D\), so \(A^k = PD^kP^{-1}\) with \(D^k = \begin{bmatrix}5^k&0\\0&2^k\end{bmatrix}\).

Spot check, \(k = 3\): entry \((1,1)\) of \(P\,\mathrm{diag}(125,8)\,P^{-1}\) is \((125\cdot2 + 8\cdot1)/3 = 86\), the \((1,1)\) entry of \(A^3\).

Power iteration

Write \(\mathbf{x}_0=2\mathbf{v}_1+\mathbf{v}_2\). Each multiplication by \(A\) scales the \(\mathbf{v}_1\) part by 5 and the \(\mathbf{v}_2\) part by 2, so almost any start swings onto the \(\lambda = 5\) direction.

Act 4 / Why it works

The biggest eigenvalue wins

Write \(\mathbf{x} = c_1\mathbf{v}_1 + c_2\mathbf{v}_2\) with \(c_1 \neq 0\). Since \(A\mathbf{v}_i = \lambda_i\mathbf{v}_i\),

$$A\mathbf{x} = c_1\lambda_1\mathbf{v}_1 + c_2\lambda_2\mathbf{v}_2$$

Apply \(A\) again and again: \(A^k\mathbf{x} = c_1\lambda_1^k\mathbf{v}_1 + c_2\lambda_2^k\mathbf{v}_2\). Factor out \(\lambda_1^k\):

$$A^k\mathbf{x} = \lambda_1^k\left(c_1\mathbf{v}_1 + c_2\left(\tfrac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1}\right)^k\mathbf{v}_2\right)$$

If \(|\lambda_1| > |\lambda_2|\) then \(|\lambda_2/\lambda_1|^k \to 0\), so the direction of \(A^k\mathbf{x}\) tends to \(\mathbf{v}_1\).
