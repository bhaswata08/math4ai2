Act 3 of 7

Computing eigenvalues

Most vectors turn when a matrix acts on them. A few only stretch.

Act 3 / Definition

An eigenvector keeps its line

$$A\,\mathbf{v} \;=\; \lambda\,\mathbf{v}, \qquad \mathbf{v} \neq \mathbf{0}$$

\(\mathbf{v}\) is an eigenvector; the stretch factor \(\lambda\) is its eigenvalue.

Which arrows keep their line?

\(A=\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\2&3\end{bmatrix}\) turns most arrows. Only \((1,1)\) and \((1,-2)\) stay on their lines, stretched by 5 and by 2.

Act 3 / Recipe

Turn it into a determinant

$$A\mathbf{v} = \lambda\mathbf{v}$$ $$\Updownarrow$$ $$(A - \lambda I)\,\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$$ $$\Updownarrow$$ $$\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$$
  • Setup: an eigenvector \(\mathbf{v}\neq\mathbf{0}\) is only scaled by \(A\). Move everything to one side.
  • Geometry: \(A-\lambda I\) sends a nonzero \(\mathbf{v}\) to \(\mathbf{0}\), so it squashes space into a lower dimension.
  • Conclusion: squashed space has zero area, so the determinant is \(0\).

Hunting for \(\det = 0\)

For \(A=\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\2&3\end{bmatrix}\): slide \(\lambda\). The parallelogram of \(A-\lambda I\) collapses at \(\lambda=2\) and \(\lambda=5\), the roots of \(p(\lambda)\). At 5, \(A-5I\) sends \(\mathbf{v}_1=(1,1)\) to \(\mathbf{0}\).

Act 3 / Worked example

Eigenvalues of \(A = \begin{bmatrix}4&1\\2&3\end{bmatrix}\)

$$\det\!\begin{bmatrix}4-\lambda&1\\2&3-\lambda\end{bmatrix} = (4-\lambda)(3-\lambda) - 2 = \lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 10$$

\(\lambda^2 - 7\lambda + 10 = (\lambda-5)(\lambda-2) = 0\), so \(\lambda_1 = 5\) and \(\lambda_2 = 2\).

Act 3 / Worked example

Eigenvector for \(\lambda = 5\)

Subtract \(\lambda = 5\) from the main diagonal of \(A\):

$$A - 5I = \begin{bmatrix} 4 - 5 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 - 5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix}$$

Set up \((A - 5I)\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}\) with \(\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}\):

$$\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ 2 & -2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

This gives the system \(-x + y = 0 \implies y = x\), and \(2x - 2y = 0 \implies y = x\) (redundant equation). Choosing \(x = 1\) gives \(y = 1\), so

$$\mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
Act 3 / Worked example

Eigenvector for \(\lambda = 2\)

Subtract \(\lambda = 2\) from the main diagonal of \(A\):

$$A - 2I = \begin{bmatrix} 4 - 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 3 - 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Set up \((A - 2I)\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}\):

$$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

This gives \(2x + y = 0 \implies y = -2x\). Choosing \(x = 1\) gives \(y = -2\), so

$$\mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}$$
Act 3 / Worked example

Eigenvectors: the null space of \(A - \lambda I\)

\(\lambda = 5\): \(\begin{bmatrix}-1&1\\2&-2\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}\), so \(\mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}\).

\(\lambda = 2\): \(\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\2&1\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}\), so \(\mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix}1\\-2\end{bmatrix}\).

Check: \(A\mathbf{v}_1 = (5,5) = 5\mathbf{v}_1\) and \(A\mathbf{v}_2 = (2,-4) = 2\mathbf{v}_2\).

Act 3 / Why the checks hold

Trace and determinant: the setup

Let \(A\) be a general \(2 \times 2\) matrix:

$$A = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$$

By definition, the trace is the sum of the main diagonal entries, and the determinant is \(ad-bc\):

$$\operatorname{tr}(A) = a + d \qquad \det(A) = ad - bc$$

1. Set up the matrix \(\lambda I - A\):

$$\lambda I - A = \lambda \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} - \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \lambda - a & -b \\ -c & \lambda - d \end{pmatrix}$$
Act 3 / Why the checks hold

Trace and determinant: the derivation

2. Compute the determinant with \(\det \begin{pmatrix} p & q \\ r & s \end{pmatrix} = ps - qr\):

$$\det(\lambda I - A) = (\lambda - a)(\lambda - d) - (-b)(-c)$$

3. Expand the terms and group by powers of \(\lambda\):

$$\det(\lambda I - A) = \lambda^2 - a\lambda - d\lambda + ad - bc = \lambda^2 - (a + d)\lambda + (ad - bc)$$

4. Substitute trace and determinant. The coefficient of \(\lambda\) is \(-(a+d) = -\operatorname{tr}(A)\). The constant term is \(ad-bc = \det(A)\). Therefore:

$$\det(\lambda I - A) = \lambda^2 - \operatorname{tr}(A)\lambda + \det(A) = 0$$
Act 3 / Two free checks

Trace and determinant

We derived

$$\det(\lambda I - A) = \lambda^2 - \operatorname{tr}(A)\lambda + \det(A) = 0$$

The eigenvalues \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\) are the roots of this polynomial, so it must also factor as

$$(\lambda - \lambda_1)(\lambda - \lambda_2) = \lambda^2 - (\lambda_1 + \lambda_2)\lambda + \lambda_1\lambda_2$$

Both lines are the same polynomial, so they agree for every \(\lambda\). Two polynomials that agree everywhere have equal coefficients.

Act 3 / Two free checks

Match the coefficients

$$\lambda^2 - \operatorname{tr}(A)\,\lambda + \det(A)$$ $$\lambda^2 - (\lambda_1 + \lambda_2)\,\lambda + \lambda_1\lambda_2$$
  • Coefficient of \(\lambda\): \(\operatorname{tr}A = \lambda_1 + \lambda_2\)
  • Constant term: \(\det A = \lambda_1\lambda_2\)

Here \(4+3 = 7 = 5+2\) and \(4\cdot3 - 1\cdot2 = 10 = 5\cdot 2\).

Always run these two checks before trusting your eigenvalues.

Act 3 / Three by three

Same recipe, bigger polynomial

For an \(n \times n\) matrix, \(\det(A-\lambda I)\) is a degree-\(n\) polynomial in \(\lambda\).

For \(3\times3\) it is a cubic: find its roots, then solve \((A-\lambda I)\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}\) once per root.

Up to \(n\) eigenvalues, counted with repeats.
