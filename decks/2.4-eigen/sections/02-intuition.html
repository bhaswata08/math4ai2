Eigenvectors: the intuition

Most vectors get turned. A few do not.

Turn a unit vector all the way round

\(A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}\). \(Ax\) lies on the dotted line through \(x\) only twice, along \((1,1)\) with stretch 3 and along \((1,-1)\) with stretch 1.

A matrix usually knocks a vector off its line

\(\begin{bmatrix}1.5&0.7\\0.6&0.8\end{bmatrix}\): the dotted line is where the vector started. It ends elsewhere.

Some directions stay on their line

Dots on the two special lines slide along them. They stretch but do not turn.

The stretch is the eigenvalue

For \(\begin{bmatrix}2.1&-0.5\\-1.8&1.2\end{bmatrix}\) one vector is stretched by 2.7, the other shrinks to 0.6. Each stays on its own line.

Act 2 / Definition

\(Av=\lambda v\)

$$A\,\underbrace{v}_{\text{eigenvector}}=\underbrace{\lambda}_{\text{eigenvalue}}\,v$$
  • \(v\neq0\) keeps its line. \(\lambda\) is the stretch along that line.
  • \(\lambda<0\) flips \(v\) to the other side; \(\lambda=0\) crushes it to the origin.
Act 2 / Read them off

Special matrices, special eigenvectors

Diagonal

The eigenvectors are the axes. The eigenvalues are the diagonal entries.

Projection

\(\lambda=1\) along the line (kept). \(\lambda=0\) across it (crushed).

Rotation

A 2D rotation by an angle other than 0 or 180 degrees turns every vector. It has no real eigenvectors.
