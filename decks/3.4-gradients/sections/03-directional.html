Directional derivative

Not just along \(x\) or \(y\): the slope in any direction you choose.

25 min

Turn the direction, watch the slope

The slope along \(\mathbf{u}\) is \(\nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u}\): largest along the gradient, zero along the contour.

Act 3 / Why the gradient is steepest

A dot product with a unit vector

$$D_{\mathbf{u}} f = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u} = |\nabla f|\,|\mathbf{u}|\cos\theta = |\nabla f|\cos\theta$$

Along the gradient

\(\theta = 0\): slope \(+|\nabla f|\), the steepest climb.

Along a contour

\(\theta = 90^\circ\): slope 0.

Against the gradient

\(\theta = 180^\circ\): the steepest descent.

\(\mathbf{u}\) must be a unit vector. For a direction \(\mathbf{v}\), use \(\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{v}/|\mathbf{v}|\).

Gradient and directional derivative

  1. Compute \(\nabla f\) at the point \((1, 3, -2)\).
