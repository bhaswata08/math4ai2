Act 2 of 5

The gradient

Stack the partial derivatives into one vector. It points uphill.

Act 2 / Definition

One partial per input

$$\nabla f(\mathbf{x}) = \begin{bmatrix} \partial f/\partial x_1 \\ \partial f/\partial x_2 \\ \vdots \\ \partial f/\partial x_n \end{bmatrix}$$

Each entry

\(\partial f/\partial x_i\): the slope of \(f\) along axis \(i\).

One vector

Same length as \(\mathbf{x}\), so it lives in the input space.

How to read it

Direction: uphill. Length: steepness.

Input: \(n\) numbers. Output: one number. The gradient is a vector in the input space.

On the surface

The surface is \(f(x,y) = x^2 + 3xy\), at \(p = (1,2)\). The \(x\) and \(y\) partials, drawn from \(p\), add up to \(\nabla f = (8,3)\), which points uphill.

On the map

Slice the bowl \(f(x,y) = x^2 + 4y^2\) at \(f = 1, 4, 9\) and push the rings down: that is the contour map. Each arrow crosses its contour at a right angle, and where contours are tight it is long: \(|\nabla f| = 4\) at \((2,0)\), \(8\) at \((0,1)\).

Slice twice, then stack

\(f = x^2 + 3xy\) at \((1,2)\): the slice \(y=2\) has slope 8, the slice \(x=1\) has slope 3. Stacked: \(\nabla f = (8, 3)\).

Act 2 / Worked example

\(f(x, y) = x^2 + 3xy\) at \((1, 2)\)

$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 2x + 3y = \color{#8bd17c}{8}$$
$$\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 3x = \color{#e8798a}{3}$$
$$\nabla f(1, 2) = \begin{bmatrix} 8 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad |\nabla f| = \sqrt{73} \approx 8.54$$

Walk in direction \((8, 3)\) to climb fastest; the climb rate there is about 8.54 per unit step.
