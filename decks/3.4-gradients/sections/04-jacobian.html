The Jacobian

Many inputs, many outputs. Up close, the map is a matrix.

A map that bends space

\(F(x, y) = \big(x + \tfrac12\sin y,\ y + \tfrac12\sin x\big)\). Grid lines curve, so no single matrix does this.

Zoom in

Near one point the grid stays straight and evenly spaced: a linear map, just like the stretch in Act 1.

Its columns

Column 1 is where a small \(x\)-step lands, column 2 where a small \(y\)-step lands.

The determinant: local area scaling

For the \(F\) above, \(J = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \tfrac12\cos y \\ \tfrac12\cos x & 1 \end{bmatrix}\), so \(\det J = 1 - \tfrac14\cos x\cos y\). A unit square becomes a parallelogram of that area: \(\approx 0.88\) at \(p=(1,0.5)\), \(1.25\) at \((\pi,0)\). Practice 3 asks for the same quantity for another map.

Rows and columns of \(J\)

\(F(x,y,z) = (xy,\ yz)\) at \((1,2,3)\): row 1 is \(\nabla F_1\); column 2 says how \(F_1, F_2\) respond to a nudge in \(y\).

Act 4 / In general

\(F: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m\)

$$J = \begin{bmatrix} \partial F_1/\partial x_1 & \cdots & \partial F_1/\partial x_n \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \partial F_m/\partial x_1 & \cdots & \partial F_m/\partial x_n \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$$

A row

Row \(i\) is the gradient of output \(F_i\), laid flat.

A column

Column \(j\): how every output moves when input \(x_j\) is nudged.

One output

With \(m = 1\) there is one row: the Jacobian is \(\nabla f^{\top}\).

\(F(\mathbf{x} + d\mathbf{x}) \approx F(\mathbf{x}) + J\,d\mathbf{x}\): the same nudge rule as Act 1 of Block 3.3, with a matrix as the rate.
