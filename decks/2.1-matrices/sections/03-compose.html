Act 3 / Order

\(AB\) means "\(B\) first, then \(A\)"

$$(AB)\,\mathbf{v} = A\,(B\,\mathbf{v})$$

Read the product right to left, the way you read \(f(g(x))\).

Act 3 / Rule

Row times column

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1\cdot 0 + 2\cdot 1 & 1\cdot 1 + 2\cdot 5 \\ 3\cdot 0 + 4\cdot 1 & 3\cdot 1 + 4\cdot 5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 11 \\ 4 & 23 \end{bmatrix}$$

Entry \((i, j)\) is row \(i\) of the left matrix dotted with column \(j\) of the right.

Act 3 / Shapes

The shape rule

$$(m \times \color{#8bd17c}{n})\,(\color{#8bd17c}{n} \times p) = (m \times p)$$

The inner sizes must match, and then they disappear.

A \(3\times 2\) times a \(2\times 4\) is fine. Reverse them and it is undefined.

Act 3 / Properties

What holds, what does not

  • Associative: \((AB)C = A(BC)\)
  • Distributive: \(A(B + C) = AB + AC\)
  • Identity: \(AI = IA = A\)
  • Not commutative: usually \(AB \neq BA\)
Act 3 / In AI

Stacked linear layers collapse

$$W_3\,(W_2\,(W_1\,\mathbf{x})) = (W_3 W_2 W_1)\,\mathbf{x}$$

Without an activation between them, three layers are one matrix.

Nonlinearities are what make depth worth having.
