A matrix moves vectors

Feed in a vector, get a vector out. Do it to every point at once.

One vector in, one vector out

The matrix takes the arrow \(\mathbf{v}\) and returns a new arrow \(A\mathbf{v}\).

Now every vector

Each arrow is moved by the same matrix. Twenty-four arrows, one matrix. Each keeps its place in the circle as the fan stretches.

Every point, one matrix

Draw each vector as a dot. For \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\), every dot \(\mathbf{p}\) goes to \(A\mathbf{p}\), and \((1, 2)\) lands on \((5, 2)\).

Columns are where \(\hat\imath\) and \(\hat\jmath\) land

Column 1 is the new \(\hat\imath\). Column 2 is the new \(\hat\jmath\).

Mix the columns

For \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\): \(1\cdot(2,1)\) + \(2\cdot(-1,1)\) = \((0,3)\).

The amounts scale the columns

For \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(\mathbf{v} = (2,3)\): \(2\cdot(2,1)\) + \(3\cdot(-1,1)\) = \((1,5)\).

Act 2 / Formula

\(A\mathbf{v}\) is a mix of columns

Write \(\mathbf{v} = x\,\hat\imath + y\,\hat\jmath\). Then

$$A\mathbf{v} = A(x\,\hat\imath + y\,\hat\jmath) = x\,(A\hat\imath) + y\,(A\hat\jmath)$$

\(A\hat\imath\) is column 1 and \(A\hat\jmath\) is column 2, so

$$\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = x\,\color{#8bd17c}{\begin{bmatrix} a \\ c \end{bmatrix}} + y\,\color{#e8798a}{\begin{bmatrix} b \\ d \end{bmatrix}}$$

The entries of \(\mathbf{v}\) are the amounts of each column.

Act 2 / Linear

What "linear" means

Let \(T\) send vectors to vectors, e.g. \(T(\mathbf{v}) = A\mathbf{v}\). \(T\) is linear when

$$T(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = T(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v}) \qquad T(c\,\mathbf{v}) = c\,T(\mathbf{v})$$

In the picture: grid lines stay straight, stay parallel, stay evenly spaced, and the origin stays put.

Every matrix is a linear map, and every linear map of the plane is a matrix.
