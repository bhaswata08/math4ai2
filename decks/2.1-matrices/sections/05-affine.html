Affine maps

Add a shift after the matrix. The origin no longer stays put.

Matrix, then shift

With \(\mathbf{y} = A\mathbf{x}\) the origin stays. With \(+\,\mathbf{b}\) it moves to \(\mathbf{b}\).

Act 5 / Definition

Affine is not linear

$$\mathbf{y} = A\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b} \qquad A\mathbf{0} + \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{b} \neq \mathbf{0}$$

A linear map must send \(\mathbf{0}\) to \(\mathbf{0}\). With \(\mathbf{b} \neq \mathbf{0}\) this one does not.

A dense layer is affine, then a nonlinearity: \(\sigma(W\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b})\).

Act 5 / One matrix

Can a shift be a matrix?

Not with the same size: every matrix \(M\) sends \(\mathbf{0}\) to \(M\mathbf{0} = \mathbf{0}\), but a shift moves \(\mathbf{0}\) to \(\mathbf{b}\).

We still want one: then a chain of affine maps is just a product of matrices.

The trick: give every point an extra coordinate that is always \(1\).

Act 5 / One matrix

A shift by \(\mathbf{b} = (3, 1)\)

Effectively: add \(3\) to \(x\) and \(1\) to \(y\). Write the point \((x, y)\) as \((x, y, 1)\) and put \(\mathbf{b}\) in the third column:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x + 3 \cdot 1 \\ y + 1 \cdot 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x + 3 \\ y + 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

The third column multiplies the constant \(1\), so it adds \(\mathbf{b}\). The last row keeps the \(1\) for the next map.

Act 5 / One matrix

Homogeneous coordinates

Replace the identity block with any \(A\). The same steps give the general affine map:

$$\begin{bmatrix} A & \mathbf{b} \\ \mathbf{0}^\top & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{x} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} A\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b} \cdot 1 \\ \mathbf{0}^\top\mathbf{x} + 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} A\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b} \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

\((\mathbf{x}, 1)\) is \(\mathbf{x}\) in homogeneous coordinates. Graphics and robotics chain every move this way.
