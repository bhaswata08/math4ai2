The smallest network

One neuron per layer, so every quantity is a single number.

What depends on what

A nudge to \(w^{(L)}\) moves \(z\), which moves \(a\), which moves the cost.

Three local factors

Multiply the factors along the path: the chain rule from Block 3.3.

Put numbers in

Multiply the three factors, then nudge \(w\) by 0.01 and check: the cost moves by the product times 0.01.

One layer further back

Each layer reuses the factor already computed for the layer after it. That reuse is the whole trick.

Act 3 / With many neurons

The same equations, in matrix form

$$\boldsymbol\delta^{(L)} = \nabla_{\mathbf a} C \odot \sigma'(\mathbf z^{(L)}) \qquad \boldsymbol\delta^{(l)} = \big(W^{(l+1)\top}\boldsymbol\delta^{(l+1)}\big) \odot \sigma'(\mathbf z^{(l)})$$ $$\frac{\partial C}{\partial W^{(l)}} = \boldsymbol\delta^{(l)}\,\mathbf a^{(l-1)\top} \qquad \frac{\partial C}{\partial \mathbf b^{(l)}} = \boldsymbol\delta^{(l)}$$

\(\odot\) is the Hadamard product from Block 2.1. The transpose is Act 2's \(W^{\top}\boldsymbol\delta\).

The matrix form, with numbers

Step back through \(W^\top\), multiply entry by entry with \(\sigma'\), then the weight gradient is \(\boldsymbol\delta\,\mathbf a^\top\), an outer product.
