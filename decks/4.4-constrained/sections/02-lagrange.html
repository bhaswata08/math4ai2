Act 2 of 4

Lagrange multipliers

Turn "parallel gradients" into equations you can solve.

25 min

Solve the system

Three equations, three unknowns: \(x\), \(y\) and the multiplier \(\lambda\).

Act 2 / The recipe

Maximise \(f(\mathbf x)\) subject to \(g(\mathbf x) = c\)

$$\mathcal L(\mathbf x, \lambda) = f(\mathbf x) - \lambda\,\big(g(\mathbf x) - c\big)$$
$$\nabla_{\mathbf x}\mathcal L = \mathbf 0,\qquad \frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial \lambda} = 0$$

Solve, then compare \(f\) at every solution: the equations find minima and maxima alike.

Act 2 / Worked example

Largest rectangle with perimeter 4

$$\max\; xy \quad \text{s.t.}\quad 2x + 2y = 4$$
  1. Gradients: \(\nabla f = (y,\, x)\) and \(\nabla g = (2,\, 2)\).
  2. \(\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g\) gives \(y = 2\lambda\) and \(x = 2\lambda\), so \(x = y\).
  3. Constraint: \(2x + 2y = 4\) gives \(x + y = 2\), so \(x = y = 1\).
  4. \(\lambda = \tfrac12\): the best rectangle is the unit square, area \(1\).

What \(\lambda\) means

\(\lambda\) is the rate at which the best value improves per unit of constraint relaxed: the shadow price.

Act 2 / Where this shows up

Constraints in machine learning

PCA

Maximise \(\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w\) with \(\lVert\mathbf w\rVert = 1\). The Lagrange condition is \(\Sigma\mathbf w = \lambda\mathbf w\): an eigenvector (Block 3.2).

SVM

Maximise the margin subject to every point being classified correctly.

Probabilities

Maximum entropy subject to \(\sum_i p_i = 1\) and a fixed expected score gives the softmax (Gibbs) form.
