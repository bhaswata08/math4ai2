Lagrange multipliers

Turn "parallel gradients" into equations you can solve.

Act 2 / The recipe

Maximise \(f(\mathbf x)\) subject to \(g(\mathbf x) = c\)

\(f\) is the objective, \(g(\mathbf x) = c\) the constraint, and \(\lambda\) the multiplier, one number per constraint.

$$\mathcal L(\mathbf x, \lambda) = f(\mathbf x) - \lambda\,\big(g(\mathbf x) - c\big)$$
$$\nabla_{\mathbf x}\mathcal L = \nabla f - \lambda\nabla g = \mathbf 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \nabla f = \lambda\nabla g$$ $$\frac{\partial \mathcal L}{\partial \lambda} = -(g - c) = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; g = c$$

Solve, then compare \(f\) at every solution: the equations find minima and maxima alike.

Act 2 / Worked example, step 1 of 4

Set up: the saddle on the circle

$$\text{maximise and minimise}\;\; f(x, y) = xy + 1 \quad \text{s.t.}\quad g(x, y) = x^2 + y^2 - 1 = 0$$
  1. \(\nabla f = \big(\partial f/\partial x,\; \partial f/\partial y\big) = (y,\; x)\)
  2. \(\nabla g = \big(\partial g/\partial x,\; \partial g/\partial y\big) = (2x,\; 2y)\)

The unknowns are \(x\), \(y\) and the multiplier \(\lambda\).

Act 2 / Worked example, step 2 of 4

Three equations, three unknowns

\(\nabla f = \lambda\nabla g\) is an equation between two vectors, so it holds in each component. The constraint adds the third.

$$y = \lambda\,(2x)$$first components: \(\partial f/\partial x = \lambda\,\partial g/\partial x\)
$$x = \lambda\,(2y)$$second components: \(\partial f/\partial y = \lambda\,\partial g/\partial y\)
$$x^2 + y^2 = 1$$the constraint \(g = 0\), still in force
Act 2 / Worked example, step 3 of 4

Solve: two cases

$$y = 2\lambda(2\lambda y) = 4\lambda^2 y \;\Rightarrow\; y\,(1 - 4\lambda^2) = 0$$put the second equation into the first
$$y = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 2\lambda y = 0$$case 1: but \(0^2 + 0^2 \ne 1\), so \((0, 0)\) is not on the circle. Reject.
$$\lambda = \pm\tfrac12 \;\Rightarrow\; x = \pm y$$case 2: \(x = 2\lambda y\) gives \(x = y\) when \(\lambda = \tfrac12\), \(x = -y\) when \(\lambda = -\tfrac12\)
$$x^2 + x^2 = 1 \;\Rightarrow\; x = \pm\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$$substitute \(y = \pm x\) into the constraint

Four candidates: \(\big(\pm\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}, \pm\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big)\), every combination of signs.

The equations only find candidates; they do not say which is a maximum. Evaluating \(f\) sorts them, and matches the two highs and two lows of Act 1.

Second example: a bowl cut by a wall

Minimise \(J = x_1^2 + x_2^2\) on the line \(x_1 + x_2 + 3 = 0\). The wall over the line cuts the bowl in a parabola; its lowest point is the answer, \(J = 4.5\).

The same picture: a level curve touches

Where a level curve only touches the line, \(\nabla J\) lies along \(\nabla c\): \(\nabla J = -3\,\nabla c\), so \(\lambda = -3\).

The Lagrangian does the bookkeeping

Setting every partial of \(\mathcal L\) to zero gives \(\nabla J = \lambda\nabla c\) and the constraint, in one system.

What \(\lambda\) means

Move the wall by \(t\) and the best value changes at rate \(\lambda\): the shadow price of the constraint.

Act 2 / Where this shows up

Constraints in machine learning

PCA: maximise \(\mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w\) with \(\lVert\mathbf w\rVert = 1\), where \(\Sigma\) is the covariance matrix (Block 3.2).

$$\mathcal L = \mathbf w^{\top}\Sigma\mathbf w - \lambda(\mathbf w^{\top}\mathbf w - 1)$$ $$\nabla\mathcal L = 2\Sigma\mathbf w - 2\lambda\mathbf w = \mathbf 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \Sigma\mathbf w = \lambda\mathbf w$$

So \(\mathbf w\) is an eigenvector of \(\Sigma\), and the maximum picks the top one (Block 3.2).

The same tool: the support vector machine (maximise the margin subject to correct classification), and the softmax (maximum entropy subject to probabilities summing to 1).
