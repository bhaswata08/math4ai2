Act 1 of 5

The determinant

One number: how much the map scales area, or volume.

Scale by 2: area times 4

\(2I\) doubles both sides, so every region covers 4 times the area.

Shear versus scale

The shear changes the shape and keeps the area at 1. Scaling by 2 keeps the shape and multiplies the area by 4.

Act 1 / Idea

Determinant as an area factor

The unit square has area 1. Apply \(A\). The image has area \(|\det A|\):

$$\det A = \text{signed area factor}$$

Every region is scaled by the same factor, whatever its shape.

\(\det(2I) = 4\) and \(\det\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix} = 1\).

Every area times 6

For \(\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\0&2\end{bmatrix}\) the unit square becomes a parallelogram of area 6, and so does everything else.

Act 1 / 2×2

The 2×2 formula

$$\det\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix} = ad - bc$$

Example: \(\det\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\2&3\end{bmatrix} = 4\cdot 3 - 1\cdot 2 = 10\).

The next clip shows where \(ad - bc\) comes from.

Why \(ad - bc\)

Green arrow \((a,c)\), rose arrow \((b,d)\). The box is \((a+b)\times(c+d) = 25\). Cut away two green triangles (\(ac\)), two rose triangles (\(bd\)) and two violet rectangles (\(2bc\)): \(25 - 8 - 3 - 4 = 10\).

A negative determinant

\(\begin{bmatrix}3&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}\) flips the picture over: area times 3, orientation reversed.

Act 1 / Sign

Size and sign

\(|\det A|\)

How much areas (or volumes) are scaled.

Sign of \(\det A\)

Negative means the map flips orientation, like a mirror.

Determinant zero

\(\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\4&2\end{bmatrix}\) has \(\det = 2\cdot2 - 1\cdot4 = 0\). The plane collapses onto the line \(y = 2x\).

Volume in 3D

First matrix: zeros below the diagonal, so \(\det = 2\cdot1\cdot3 = 6\), the new volume. Second matrix: row 3 = row 1 + row 2, so \(\det = 0\) and the cube flattens to a plane.

Cofactor walk: a 3×3 determinant

Strike row 1 and one column, take the 2×2 determinant, multiply by the entry and the sign. Sum: 20 - 0 + (-2) = 18.

Act 1 / 3×3

Cofactor expansion

Walk along the first row. Each entry multiplies the determinant of what is left after deleting its row and column. Signs alternate \(+\,-\,+\).

$$\det A = a_{11}M_{11} - a_{12}M_{12} + a_{13}M_{13}$$

\(M_{1j}\) is the 2×2 determinant left after deleting row 1 and column \(j\).

Act 1 / Worked example

A 3×3 determinant

$$\det\begin{bmatrix}2&0&1\\1&3&2\\1&1&4\end{bmatrix} = 2\begin{vmatrix}3&2\\1&4\end{vmatrix} - 0\begin{vmatrix}1&2\\1&4\end{vmatrix} + 1\begin{vmatrix}1&3\\1&1\end{vmatrix}$$

\(= 2(12-2) - 0 + 1(1-3) = 20 - 2 = 18\)

A zero entry kills its whole term. Expand along the row or column with the most zeros.

Act 1 / Properties

Four rules

Products

\(\det(AB) = \det A\,\det B\). \(B\) scales areas by \(\det B\), then \(A\) scales by \(\det A\).

Transpose

\(\det(A^{\mathsf T}) = \det A\). A fact: rows and columns span the same area.

Scalar

\(\det(cA) = c^{n}\det A\) for \(n\times n\). Each of the \(n\) columns scales by \(c\). Check: \(\det(2I_2) = 4\).

Inverse

\(\det(A^{-1}) = 1/\det A\), when \(A^{-1}\) exists. Since \(1 = \det I = \det(AA^{-1}) = \det A\cdot\det A^{-1}\).
